In Japan, families can use a combination of cash and cards, but it's essential to carry cash for many small shops and public transport. Imagine your toddler getting hangry at a vending machine while you fumble for yen!

Cash vs. Cards

Japan is known for its cash-centric culture. While urban areas like Tokyo and Osaka are increasingly card-friendly, many smaller establishments still prefer cash. ATMs that accept foreign cards are typically located in convenience stores like 7-Eleven or FamilyMart, making it easy to withdraw cash when needed. Keep in mind that exchanging currency at banks or airports may come with high fees, so it’s wise to bring some yen with you or use ATMs directly.

ATMs may have transaction limits, often around ¥100,000 (about $900). And remember, while some places may accept a credit card, don’t be surprised if you encounter a "cash only" sign.

Budgeting for Family Meals

Your food budget can vary significantly based on your family's preferences. A meal at a casual restaurant might cost around ¥1,000-¥2,500 per person, while dining at a more upscale establishment can run ¥5,000 or more. For families, convenience stores offer an array of affordable, kid-friendly meal options, such as onigiri (rice balls) starting at around ¥150, and bento boxes for ¥500-¥1,200, which you can enjoy on the go. Don’t forget to pack a few snacks for the inevitable "I’m starving!" moments.

Dining out with kids can be a fun adventure, but it’s good to have a budget in mind for meals each day. A practical approach is to allocate around ¥3,000-¥5,000 per person for daily food, though you can adjust this based on your family’s dining style.

Getting Around: Transport Costs

Transportation is another key aspect of budgeting in Japan. If you're planning to use the train system frequently, consider getting a Japan Rail Pass (from ¥29,650 for a 7-day pass) or a regional pass if you’re staying in one area. The pass allows unlimited travel on JR trains, which is a significant cost-saver, especially for families. For local travel, prepaid cards like Suica or Pasmo allow for easy tap-and-go access on trains and buses, making it simple to hop on and off.

Tips for Managing Money

Here are some practical tips for handling money while traveling in Japan with kids:

  • Keep a mix of cash and cards handy, but lean towards cash for smaller shops.
  • Use a prepaid Suica or Pasmo card for convenience on public transport.
  • Budget around ¥3,000-¥5,000 per person daily for meals and snacks.
  • Plan for attraction ticket costs; for example, a family ticket to Tokyo Disneyland costs around ¥16,000 (as of 2026, confirm on the official site).

With these budgeting tips, you can ensure that your family enjoys the culinary delights and cultural experiences Japan has to offer without breaking the bank!

3. How much should I budget for meals in Japan?

Plan for around ¥3,000-¥5,000 per person daily for meals.
