Traveling around Japan with kids is easy thanks to its efficient public transport system. From child-friendly IC cards to stroller-friendly trains and buses, you’ll find everything you need to make your journey smooth. Picture your little ones giggling as they spot Hello Kitty on the train! Here’s how to navigate Japan’s public transport with your family.

  • IC cards simplify fare payments.
  • Child fares are available for various ages.
  • Buses and subways are stroller-friendly.
  • Nursing rooms and diaper-changing facilities are common.
  • Local discounts for kids often apply.

TL;DR

  • Use child IC cards (Kodomo Suica, PASMO, ICOCA) for discounted fares.
  • Infants under 1 year old travel free; toddlers can travel free with restrictions.
  • Most public transport is stroller-friendly, but check specific policies.
  • Nursing rooms are widely available at major stations.

IC Cards for Kids

IC cards like Kodomo Suica, Kodomo PASMO, and Kodomo ICOCA are essential for family travel. These child-specific cards automatically apply a reduced fare, typically half of the adult fare, when tapped at ticket gates. You can easily register these cards with your child's name and date of birth, helping to prevent misuse and allowing for hassle-free re-issuance if lost. They are generally available for children aged 6 to under 12 or until elementary school graduation.

To purchase a child IC card, visit a station ticket vending machine or counter. You will need to provide your child's name and date of birth. When traveling with your child, if they have their own IC card, they can tap it themselves. If not, the adult can tap their own card while the child passes through the gates with them. For safety, if the child is using a regular ticket, opt for the wide ticket gates or ask staff for assistance.

Child Fares

Japan Railways (JR) offers free travel for infants under 1 year old. Toddlers aged 1 to before elementary school entry can travel free with up to two toddlers per fare-paying adult. For the third toddler onward, a child fare is required. If they occupy their own seat, a child fare is necessary. Children aged 6-11, or up to 12 if still in elementary school, pay half the adult fare.

On Tokyo Toei buses, the adult fare is 210 yen (as of 2026), while the child fare is 110 yen (IC card: 105 yen). Infants travel free, and toddlers follow similar rules as JR. Payment can be made with cash or IC cards. Board from the front and exit from the middle or rear door.

Stroller Access and Policy

Traveling with a stroller? No problem! Strollers can be brought onto Shinkansen trains free of charge. It’s recommended to reserve seats with oversized luggage space, allowing you to keep the stroller unfolded during the journey. If your stroller exceeds 160 cm in total dimensions, you will need to reserve a designated luggage space.

For buses, as of June 2021, two-person strollers can remain unfolded on all Toei Bus routes, while single-person strollers can be secured at designated seats. Remember, strollers need to be folded if they are too wide or if the bus is crowded. Always check with the bus staff if you’re unsure about the rules for your specific route.

Nursing Rooms and Diaper-Changing Facilities

Finding a place to change or nurse your little one is straightforward in Japan. Many Tokyo Metro stations feature facilities for infants, including nursing rooms and diaper-changing stations. For example, stations like Shinjuku and Tokyo Station have well-equipped nursing rooms. You can find a list of these stations on the Tokyo Metro website. Additionally, the Kodomo Smile Spot program in select stations provides extra amenities for families, such as play areas and family restrooms.

Booking the Shinkansen with a family: a step-by-step

“Take the bullet train” is the easy part. Here is how families actually book it with little kids, big suitcases and a stroller. Fares and rules change — confirm on the official sites before you travel (as of 2026).

  1. Reserve seats — don’t wing it with kids. Book reserved seats in English on the official apps: smartEX for the Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu Shinkansen (Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka–Hiroshima) or EKINET for JR East. You can also book at a JR ticket office (Midori-no-madoguchi) or a green ticket machine. Reserve a few days ahead in peak seasons.
  2. Book the oversized-baggage seats for big suitcases. On the Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu Shinkansen, a bag whose height + width + depth totals 160–250 cm needs a free “oversized baggage” seat reservation — the last-row seats, with a storage area behind them. Reserving it costs nothing extra; turning up without one is a ¥1,000 surcharge on board. Bags over 250 cm aren’t allowed.
  3. Strollers: book the last row. Fold the stroller and store it in the space behind the rear seats, so reserve the last row of the car. A folded stroller doesn’t need its own reservation.
  4. Know the kids’ fares. Up to two children under 6 ride free per paying adult if they sit on your lap; if a young child takes their own seat, you pay a child fare. Children aged 6–11 pay half the adult fare with their own reserved seat.
  5. Budget roughly. A Tokyo–Kyoto reserved seat is around ¥14,000 per adult one way, so two adults + one 6–11 child is roughly ¥35,000 one way — confirm exact fares for your dates on the official site. A Japan Rail Pass can be cheaper if you take several long trips.
  6. On the day. Arrive 20–30 minutes early, use station elevators (most are stroller-accessible), and grab ekiben (station bento boxes) so hungry kids are sorted for the ride.

FAQ

Are IC cards necessary for traveling in Japan with kids?

While not mandatory, IC cards make travel easier and faster, especially when using trains and buses.

What should I do if my child loses their IC card?

If your child's IC card is lost, visit a ticket counter for re-issuance using your registration details.

Are public transport systems stroller-friendly?

Yes, most public transport systems in Japan, including trains and buses, accommodate strollers with specific policies in place.

Can I get discounts for my kids on public transport?

Yes, children typically enjoy discounted fares, and infants may travel free under certain conditions.

Where can I find nursing rooms in train stations?

Nursing rooms are commonly found in major train stations; check the respective railway websites for specific locations.

What if I have more than two toddlers?

For families with more than two toddlers, child fares will apply for the third and subsequent toddlers on most transport systems.

Are there stroller rental services in Japan?

Yes, stroller rental services like Babycal are available at various train stations in Japan, including Tokyo Metro stations.

How do I pay for bus fares?

Bus fares can be paid with cash or transportation IC cards. Ensure to have small bills, as larger denominations might not be accepted.

With a little preparation and knowledge, navigating Japan's public transport with kids can be a breeze. Keep this guide handy to make your travels smoother and more enjoyable!
