Traveling in Japan with kids just got easier: luggage forwarding services, known as 'Takuhaibin,' allow families to send their bags directly from airports, hotels, or even convenience stores to their next destination. This means you can focus on your children, whether it’s holding hands, pushing strollers, or enjoying a snack, instead of struggling with heavy suitcases.

TL;DR: Hands-Free Family Travel in Japan

  • Luggage forwarding (Takuhaibin) lets you send bags, strollers, and car seats between hotels, airports, and even stores.
  • Yamato Transport (Kuroneko Yamato) and Sagawa Express are the two main, reliable providers.
  • Typical costs range from ¥1,500 to ¥3,000 per standard suitcase.
  • Most deliveries are next-day; plan for 1-2 days, especially for longer distances or peak times.
  • Fill out forms carefully, including your destination's exact address and booking name.

Why Luggage Forwarding is a Parent's Best Friend

Picture this: you've just landed in Japan after a long flight with a jet-lagged toddler, and instead of wrestling multiple suitcases onto a crowded train, you breeze through customs with just your carry-ons. This is a reality for many families in Japan, thanks to their efficient luggage forwarding services. With kids in tow, whether they’re in strollers or running around, reducing the amount of luggage you carry can turn a stressful trip into a more relaxed one. Just think about navigating bustling Tokyo stations or Kyoto’s narrow streets without the burden of heavy luggage!

How Takuhaibin Works: Sending and Receiving

Using Takuhaibin is quite simple. You drop off your luggage at any designated counter, fill out a straightforward form, pay the fee, and your bags are on their way. They will typically reach your next destination the following day, making travel a breeze. It’s almost like magic, just with tracking numbers to keep you informed.

Where to Send Your Luggage From:

  • Airports: Major international airports like Narita (NRT), Haneda (HND), and Kansai (KIX) have Takuhaibin counters available. This service is perfect for sending your bags directly from the airport to your first hotel.
  • Hotels: Most hotels, particularly mid-range and luxury accommodations, provide luggage forwarding services from their front desk. It’s often the most convenient option for families.
  • Convenience Stores: Stores like Seven-Eleven, FamilyMart, and Lawson often serve as drop-off points. Look for Takuhaibin signage; this is great for any last-minute luggage sending.
  • Depots: While Yamato and Sagawa have dedicated depots across Japan, using these may be less convenient with children in tow.

Where to Receive Your Luggage:

  • Hotels: Your hotel will typically hold your bags until you arrive. It’s a good practice to inform your hotel in advance about your expected luggage delivery.
  • Airports: You can also arrange to pick up your bags at the airport if you're leaving Japan. Be sure to verify specific counter locations and their operating hours.
  • Depots: If needed, you can pick up your luggage from depots as well.

Key Players: Yamato Transport & Sagawa Express

Japan's leading luggage forwarding companies are Yamato Transport, affectionately known as Kuroneko Yamato (Black Cat Yamato, due to its recognizable logo), and Sagawa Express. Both companies offer similar services, with slight differences in their networks and specialties. Reliability is a hallmark of both providers.

What You Can Send (and What Not To)

You can send many standard luggage items, including suitcases, duffel bags, and backpacks. For families, a significant advantage is the ability to forward items like strollers, car seats, and even ski or snowboard gear if your family plans to hit the slopes. Just imagine your 5-year-old happily exploring a temple while you don’t have to push an empty stroller around! However, be aware of restrictions: perishable goods, valuables (like cash and jewelry), fragile items (unless properly packaged and declared), and dangerous goods are generally not allowed.

If you prefer not to bring bulky baby gear from home, consider renting. You can often rent a stroller, car seat & baby gear in Japan directly, which can save you the hassle of forwarding and help reduce your luggage count.

Cost and Delivery Times: Planning Ahead

The cost of forwarding your luggage depends on the size and weight of your items, as well as the distance they need to travel. A standard large suitcase (up to 120cm combined length/width/height and weighing 20kg) typically costs between ¥1,500 and ¥3,000 (as of 2026, confirm on the official site). These prices are a worthwhile investment for the freedom you gain during your travel. Delivery times usually range from next-day service for most routes, especially between major cities. For longer distances (like Tokyo to Sapporo) or to remote areas, it may take two days. Always allow for at least one full day for delivery, and ideally two days if your schedule allows, to avoid last-minute stress. If you’re sending luggage to the airport, we recommend sending your bags at least 48 hours before your flight.

Practical Tips for Families Using Takuhaibin

  • Pack a Small Overnight Bag: Always keep essential items—medication, a change of clothes, toiletries, and immediate kid necessities (like snacks and a favorite toy)—in a carry-on or backpack. This is crucial in case of any unexpected delays with your forwarded luggage.
  • Clearly Label Everything: Use luggage tags on all items, and consider adding an internal label with your name, destination hotel, and phone number.
  • Fill Forms Meticulously: Double-check the destination address, hotel name, and your booking name. If you’re staying under a different name, make sure to note that. Providing the hotel’s phone number is also important.
  • Keep Your Receipt: Your receipt includes a tracking number, allowing you to monitor your bag's progress online, often in English.
  • Inform Your Hotel: Contact your destination hotel a day or two before sending your bags to let them know you’ll be expecting a luggage delivery. Provide your name and expected arrival date.
  • Check Service Hours: Confirm the operating hours for Takuhaibin counters, especially at airports or smaller convenience stores.

FAQ

How much does it cost to forward luggage in Japan?

A standard suitcase typically costs between ¥1,500 and ¥3,000, depending on size, weight, and distance (as of 2026, confirm on the official site).

How long does luggage forwarding take in Japan?

Most deliveries between major cities are next-day. For longer distances or remote areas, allow for two days. Always plan with a buffer.

Can I send a stroller or car seat via Takuhaibin?

Yes, oversized items like strollers and car seats can generally be forwarded. They may incur a slightly higher fee due to their size.

Where can I drop off my luggage for forwarding?

You can drop off luggage at major airports, most hotel front desks, and many convenience stores (Seven-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson), as well as dedicated depots.

Do I need to inform my hotel that I'm forwarding luggage?

Yes, it’s highly recommended to inform your destination hotel in advance that you’re expecting a luggage delivery, providing your name and estimated arrival date.

Can I track my forwarded luggage?

Yes, you will receive a tracking number on your receipt, which you can use to track your luggage online, often through English-language interfaces.

Is luggage forwarding reliable in Japan?

Japanese luggage forwarding services are renowned for their exceptional reliability and punctuality, making them a trusted option for travelers.

Using Japan’s luggage forwarding services can significantly simplify your family’s travel, allowing you to focus on making precious memories together.
