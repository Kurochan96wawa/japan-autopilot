Etiquette for Children at Temples and Shrines

Understanding basic etiquette can enhance your visit and make it more enjoyable for your kids. Here are some key points to remember:

  • Entering the Sacred Grounds: Bow once before passing through a torii (shrine gate) or sanmon (temple gate). Walk along the sides, as the center path (seichu) is reserved for the gods.
  • Hand-washing at the Temizuya: Before approaching the main hall, cleanse your hands and mouth at the temizuya (water purification fountain). Here’s how:
StepAction
1Hold the ladle in your right hand, scoop water, and pour it over your left hand.
2Switch the ladle to your left hand and pour water over your right hand.
3Hold the ladle in your right hand, cup water in your left hand, and rinse your mouth (do not let the ladle touch your mouth). Spit the water discreetly beside the fountain.
4Rinse your left hand again.
5Finally, tilt the ladle vertically to cleanse the handle and return it.
  • Worship at Shrines: Toss a coin into the saisenbako (offering box), ring the bell (if available), bow twice, clap twice, make your wish, and bow once more.
  • Worship at Temples: Similar to shrines but without clapping. Simply toss a coin, ring the bell, and pray with hands together.
  • General Behavior: Encourage children to be respectful and quiet. Running, loud talking, or excessive play is discouraged, and eating and smoking are not allowed within sacred grounds.
  • Best Time to Visit: Early morning or late morning is suggested for a more tranquil experience.

Child-Friendly Facilities and Services

Many temples and shrines cater to families, providing amenities that ensure a smoother visit:

  • Nursing Rooms / Diaper Changing Facilities:
    • Ise Jingu: Both the Geku and Naiku shrines have diaper changing facilities, with the Naiku offering a nursing room.
    • Kyoto Railway Museum: Equipped with nursing rooms that also feature facilities for preparing formula.
    • Kyoto Department Stores: Many offer facilities including hot water for formula and nursing rooms.
  • Stroller Access and Rental: Some temple grounds have gravel paths or many steps, which can be stroller-challenging. For those using strollers:

Engaging Activities for Kids

Here are some activities that can make your visit interactive and enjoyable:

  • Collecting Goshuin: Encourage older children to collect unique calligraphic stamps from each location. It’s like a treasure hunt!
  • Drawing Omikuji: Kids can draw fortune slips, often with small charms. Teach them to tie unfavorable fortunes to designated ropes or trees to leave bad luck behind.
  • Writing on Ema: Children can write wishes or draw on wooden plaques and hang them up.
  • Special Children's Programs:
    • Natsuyasumi Kodomo Jinja Taiken Gakushu: A summer program at Meiji Jingu focused on cooperation for children in grades 4-6, typically held in late July. Participation fee is around 15,000 yen; confirm details for 2026.
    • Sankatsu Taiken: Temples like Chikoin in Chiba offer activities like Zen meditation, calligraphy, and overnight stays.
  • Experiencing Shichi-Go-San: If visiting in autumn, you might see children dressed in traditional kimono celebrating this rite of passage, which is a colorful experience.

Allergy Information

Specific allergy labeling can vary across different locations in Japan. Always check with staff at restaurants or facilities about potential allergens in food or other products. Consult with a healthcare professional if you have concerns about allergies.

FAQ

What should kids wear when visiting temples and shrines?

Comfortable, respectful clothing is best. Avoid overly casual attire like shorts and flip-flops, especially in sacred areas.

Are there any kid-friendly temples in Kyoto?

Yes, temples like Kinkaku-ji and Fushimi Inari Taisha are popular with families and offer engaging experiences.

Is it necessary to speak Japanese to visit temples and shrines?

No, many signs are in English, and staff are generally accommodating to tourists.

What are the best times to visit temples and shrines in Japan?

Early morning or late afternoon is ideal to avoid crowds and enjoy a peaceful atmosphere.

Can we bring snacks into temple grounds?

Eating is typically not allowed in sacred areas, so it’s best to enjoy snacks outside the temple grounds.

Are there any special festivals for children at temples?

Yes, many temples hold seasonal festivals with activities for children, such as crafts or traditional performances.

How do we find stroller rentals?

Stroller rentals are available at major train stations and some tourist spots, like the Kyoto Railway Museum. Consider checking in advance.

What should we do if our child has allergies?

Consult with local staff regarding food allergies and always carry necessary medications.
