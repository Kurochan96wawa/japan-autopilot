Understanding the Baggage Rules

The Shinkansen allows oversized baggage, but it’s important to note the specifications. Baggage exceeding 160 cm (height + width + length) incurs a fee, usually around 1,000 yen, so be sure to check the official site for the latest information. Strollers that fold or fit within the 160 cm limit can be taken on board without additional charges. If you’re traveling with a larger stroller, utilize the designated luggage areas, typically located near the entrance of each train.

How to Reserve Seats for Your Family

To make reservations for the Shinkansen, use the official JR East website or visit ticket offices once in Japan. You can reserve seats online up to one month in advance. For families, it's advisable to book a family compartment, which accommodates up to six people. These compartments offer extra space, making it easier to manage your children and their belongings. Prices for a family compartment can vary based on the train line and time of travel, typically ranging from 8,000 to 20,000 yen (as of 2026, confirm on the official site).

Stroller-Friendly Options

Japan is known for being stroller-friendly, but it’s crucial to choose your stroller wisely. Opt for a lightweight, easily foldable stroller that fits within the 2-meter length when folded. Many families find that a compact umbrella stroller works best. If you won’t need your stroller for the entirety of your trip, consider renting a stroller in Japan to avoid the hassle of transporting one from home.

Traveling with Baby Gear

If you're traveling with infants or toddlers, you might have additional gear like car seats or baby carriers. The Shinkansen offers a limited amount of space for baby equipment, so it’s advisable to keep your belongings to a minimum. You can rent baby gear, including car seats, through services like Klook, making it easier to travel light without sacrificing safety. For any health-related concerns, consult with a pediatrician to ensure you have what you need for your child.

Tips for a Smooth Journey

1. Arrive Early: Give yourself enough time to navigate the station, especially if you have kids in tow. Arriving 30-45 minutes early is generally advisable, particularly at busy stations like Tokyo or Osaka.
2. Pack Smart: Use packing cubes or pouches to organize snacks, toys, and essentials. This helps keep your carry-on manageable and easy to access during the trip.
3. Snack Attack: Keep snacks handy—these little energy boosters can be lifesavers during the journey. Japan's vending machines offer a variety of treats, so don’t hesitate to stock up!

Booking the Shinkansen with a family: a step-by-step

“Take the bullet train” is the easy part. Here is how families actually book it with little kids, big suitcases and a stroller. Fares and rules change — confirm on the official sites before you travel (as of 2026).

  1. Reserve seats — don’t wing it with kids. Book reserved seats in English on the official apps: smartEX for the Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu Shinkansen (Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka–Hiroshima) or EKINET for JR East. You can also book at a JR ticket office (Midori-no-madoguchi) or a green ticket machine. Reserve a few days ahead in peak seasons.
  2. Book the oversized-baggage seats for big suitcases. On the Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu Shinkansen, a bag whose height + width + depth totals 160–250 cm needs a free “oversized baggage” seat reservation — the last-row seats, with a storage area behind them. Reserving it costs nothing extra; turning up without one is a ¥1,000 surcharge on board. Bags over 250 cm aren’t allowed.
  3. Strollers: book the last row. Fold the stroller and store it in the space behind the rear seats, so reserve the last row of the car. A folded stroller doesn’t need its own reservation.
  4. Know the kids’ fares. Up to two children under 6 ride free per paying adult if they sit on your lap; if a young child takes their own seat, you pay a child fare. Children aged 6–11 pay half the adult fare with their own reserved seat.
  5. Budget roughly. A Tokyo–Kyoto reserved seat is around ¥14,000 per adult one way, so two adults + one 6–11 child is roughly ¥35,000 one way — confirm exact fares for your dates on the official site. A Japan Rail Pass can be cheaper if you take several long trips.
  6. On the day. Arrive 20–30 minutes early, use station elevators (most are stroller-accessible), and grab ekiben (station bento boxes) so hungry kids are sorted for the ride.

FAQ

What is the size limit for strollers on the Shinkansen?

Strollers should be under 2 meters in length when folded to avoid oversized baggage fees.

Can I reserve seats online for the Shinkansen?

Yes, you can reserve seats online through the official JR East website or at ticket offices in Japan.

How much does it cost to reserve a family compartment?

Prices for family compartments range from 8,000 to 20,000 yen, depending on the train and time of travel (as of 2026, confirm on the official site).

Is there a fee for oversized baggage?

Yes, oversized baggage exceeding 160 cm incurs a fee of approximately 1,000 yen (as of 2026, confirm on the official site).

Can I rent baby gear in Japan?

Absolutely! You can rent strollers, car seats, and other baby gear through services like Klook.

Are there family-friendly services on the Shinkansen?

Many Shinkansen trains offer family compartments, which provide extra space and comfort for families traveling together.

What should I do if my stroller doesn’t fit?

If your stroller is too large, consider renting a stroller upon arrival or using a baby carrier for the duration of your trip.

Can I take food on the Shinkansen?

Yes, bringing your own snacks and meals is common and encouraged. Just be considerate of your fellow passengers!

By understanding these rules and tips, you can plan effectively for an enjoyable family trip on the Shinkansen. Happy travels!
