Where to Eat on a Budget

Japan is filled with eateries that cater to families without breaking the bank. Here are some of the best types of dining options you can explore:

1. Conveyor Belt Sushi (Kaiten-zushi)

A fun experience for kids, conveyor belt sushi restaurants offer a wide selection of sushi on a moving belt, allowing everyone to pick what they like. Prices typically start around 100 yen per plate, making it a delightful and affordable meal choice. Popular chains like Sushiro and Kappa Sushi can be found in major cities, and many locations have English menus.

2. Ramen Shops

Ramen is a staple in Japanese cuisine, and you'll find countless shops serving delicious bowls at reasonable prices. Many ramen shops have set meals for around 800-1,200 yen. Ichiran is famous for its tonkotsu ramen, while local favorites can often be discovered in alleyways throughout cities. Note: most ramen shops are kid-friendly, and you can often find high chairs available.

3. Family Restaurants (Fami-res)

Family restaurants like Denny's and offer a variety of Western and Japanese dishes, making them perfect for picky eaters. Meals are affordable, usually ranging from 600 to 1,200 yen. Plus, they often have children's menus that include options like spaghetti and fried chicken.

4. Convenience Stores (Konbini)

Don’t overlook Japan's convenience stores like 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, and Lawson. They are treasure troves of budget-friendly meals. You can grab onigiri (rice balls), sandwiches, or bento boxes for as little as 300-800 yen. These quick meals are perfect for a picnic in a park or while on the go.

5. Street Food Markets

Markets such as Tsukiji Outer Market in Tokyo and Nishiki Market in Kyoto offer affordable and delicious street food options. You can sample takoyaki (octopus balls) or yakitori (grilled chicken skewers) for around 200-500 yen each. Kids will love trying different snacks, and parents can enjoy a variety of tastes without overspending.

Tips for Budget Family Dining

Meal times can be a challenge with kids in tow. Here are some practical tips to make dining out easier:

  • Look for Lunch Specials: Many restaurants offer discounted lunch sets (usually served until 2 PM) that can save you a chunk of change.
  • Use the Japan Rail Pass: If traveling by train, consider getting a Japan Rail Pass. Many major train stations have affordable meal options and food courts.
  • Get Cash: While credit cards are accepted in many places, some smaller shops only take cash, so keep yen on hand.
  • Engage with Kids: Let your kids pick their meal, or have them help choose what to eat. It can make the experience fun and engaging!

FAQ

What is the average cost of family meals in Japan?

The average meal cost for a family can range from 1,000 to 3,000 yen per person depending on the restaurant type.

Are there kid-friendly dining options in Japan?

Yes, many family restaurants and fast-food chains cater specifically to children, with special menus and high chairs available.

Is it common to find vegetarian or vegan meals?

Vegetarian options are available, but they may be limited. Look for family restaurants and convenience stores for more choices.

Are meals in convenience stores safe for children?

Yes, meals from convenience stores are generally safe and of good quality, making them a convenient option for families.

Can I find gluten-free options in Japan?

Gluten-free options are available, but it's best to research specific restaurants or eateries that cater to this dietary need.

How do I find restaurants with English menus?

Many restaurants in tourist areas offer English menus. Look for signs outside or use translation apps for assistance.

Are there any food tours suitable for families?

Yes, many food tours cater to families, allowing you to sample various dishes while learning about Japanese cuisine. You can book skip-the-line family tickets & experiences on Klook.

What should I do if my child has food allergies?

Inform restaurant staff about allergies, and carry allergy translation cards to communicate specific dietary needs.

Traveling through Japan with kids doesn't have to be an expensive venture when it comes to meals. With the right dining choices and a bit of planning, your family can enjoy delicious Japanese cuisine without breaking the bank.
