Where to Buy Diapers and Baby Formula in Japan (2026)

Where to Shop

Finding diapers and baby formula in Japan is generally straightforward, especially in urban areas. Here’s where to start:

1. Convenience Stores

Convenience stores like 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, and Lawson are your best friends. They’re located nearly everywhere, including train stations and tourist hotspots. Expect to find a range of baby products, including:

Product TypeTypical Price Range (Yen)
Diapers (Pampers, Merries)600 - 1,200
Baby Formula (Meiji, Wakodo)1,000 - 3,000
Baby Wipes300 - 700

2. Drugstores

Chain pharmacies like Tsuruha Drug and Matsumoto Kiyoshi are great spots for a broader selection of baby care products. They often carry organic options, hypoallergenic formulas, and other specialty items. Prices are similar to convenience stores, but you may find bulk buying options that save on costs.

3. Supermarkets

Supermarkets such as AEON and Seiyu feature larger aisles dedicated to baby care. Here, you can explore various brands and sizes, often at more competitive prices. Look for sales on larger packs if you're staying for an extended time. Don’t forget to check the expiry dates on baby formulas!

4. Online Shopping

If you prefer shopping from the comfort of your accommodation, consider checking out online retailers like Amazon Japan or Rakuten. They offer delivery services for a wide range of baby products, though you may need to navigate language barriers. Ensure you allow enough time for delivery, especially if you’re running low on essentials.

For added convenience, you might want to rent a stroller, car seat & baby gear in Japan. This can lighten your load and let you focus on family adventures instead of lugging everything from home!

Popular Brands Explained

When it comes to diapers and baby formulas, familiarity can help ease any shopping anxiety. Here’s a rundown of the most common brands:

Diapers

Pampers: Available in various sizes, Pampers are a global favorite and offer good absorbency. Sizes range from newborn to size 6.

Merries: Known for their softness and breathability, Merries diapers come in sizes from newborn to size 6, ideal for active toddlers.

Goo.N: This brand features a unique stretchy design for comfort and movement, available in sizes that suit various stages of growth.

Baby Formula

Meiji: A trusted local brand, Meiji offers a range of formulas suitable for infants to toddlers, often enriched with DHA for brain development.

Wakodo: Known for its gentle formula, Wakodo is another popular choice among parents and has options tailored for different age groups.

Morinaga: This brand provides various formulas, including hypoallergenic options, for babies with specific needs.

Sizes and Types

Diaper sizes in Japan typically correspond to weight categories:

  • Newborn: Up to 5 kg
  • Size S: 4-8 kg
  • Size M: 6-11 kg
  • Size L: 9-14 kg
  • Size XL: 12-17 kg

Baby formulas are categorized by age, usually ranging from newborn to 36 months. Always check the packaging for specific age and weight recommendations!

FAQ

Where can I buy diapers in Japan?

You can buy diapers in convenience stores, drugstores, and supermarkets. Major chains like 7-Eleven and AEON are good options.

What brands of baby formula are available?

Popular brands include Meiji, Wakodo, and Morinaga, all of which are commonly found in stores.

Are English labels available on baby products?

Some products may have English labels, but it’s wise to familiarize yourself with the Japanese terms for sizes and ingredients.

How do Japanese diaper sizes compare to Western sizes?

Japanese diaper sizes are typically based on weight, so check the packaging for the appropriate size for your child.

Can I find organic baby products in Japan?

Yes, many drugstores and supermarkets offer organic baby products, including diapers and formulas.

Are baby products more expensive in Japan?

Prices can vary, but baby products are generally comparable to prices in Western countries, especially in urban areas.

Should I bring my own baby formula?

If your baby has specific dietary needs, it may be best to bring some from home. Otherwise, you can find many options in Japan.

What if I need emergency supplies?

Look for the nearest convenience store or pharmacy; they’re usually open late and have a decent selection of baby supplies.

In summary, purchasing diapers and baby formula in Japan is manageable, with many options available. Don’t forget to enjoy the experience of exploring these local stores with your little ones!
