They typically have locations in major cities, making it easy for traveling families to find a store nearby. Nishimatsuya (西松屋) focuses on affordable clothing and baby gear, with stores scattered across the country, including cities like Fukuoka and Sapporo.

Understanding Diaper Sizes

Diaper sizes in Japan are labeled differently than in many Western countries. Here’s a quick guide to help you choose the right size:

SizeWeight Range (kg)Equivalent Size (US)
S4-8 kgNewborn
M6-11 kgSize 1-2
L9-14 kgSize 3
XL12-20 kgSize 4

When selecting diapers, always check the weight ranges on the packaging. For example, a size M diaper is suitable for babies weighing between 6-11 kg, roughly equivalent to US sizes 1-2. This information is crucial for ensuring comfort and fit for your little one.

Baby Wipes and Formula

Baby wipes are widely accessible, with various brands available in stores. The price typically ranges from ¥300 to ¥800 for a pack (as of 2026, confirm on the official site). Popular brands include the Smart Angel line, known for their thicker, more absorbent wipes, which you can find at locations like Nishimatsuya.

As for baby formula, major brands like Meiji and Morinaga are commonly used and can be found in supermarkets and drugstores. A standard can of formula costs between ¥2,000 to ¥4,000 (as of 2026, confirm on the official site). When purchasing, check the labels for age-appropriate options, and consider bringing a translation app to help with any specific dietary needs. Always consult your pediatrician for any health-related questions regarding formula or allergies.

Language Tips for Shopping

While many Japanese people speak some English, knowing a few basic phrases can make your shopping experience smoother:

  • おむつはどこですか？ (Omutu wa doko desu ka?) - Where are the diapers?
  • おしりふきはありますか？ (Oshiri fuki wa arimasu ka?) - Do you have baby wipes?
  • ミルクはどこですか？ (Miruku wa doko desu ka?) - Where is the formula?

Having these phrases handy can significantly enhance your interactions and help you navigate store aisles more effectively.

Tax-Free Shopping

Good news for international travelers: many stores in Japan offer tax-free shopping for foreign visitors. To qualify, you must show your passport at the time of purchase, which can save you about 8% on your total bill—definitely a welcome bonus when buying baby supplies!
