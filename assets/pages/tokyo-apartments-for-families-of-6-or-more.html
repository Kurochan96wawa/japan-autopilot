Tokyo Apartments for Families of 6 or More (2026)

Booking sites stop being useful somewhere around five people. This is every Tokyo hotel room we could verify that officially takes six or more adults in one booking — from 6-bed apartments to connecting apartments for eight and nine, and one hotel that rents you a whole floor for twelve. Room names, floor areas, bed counts and occupancy come from each hotel’s own website.

How this list was made. We read the room pages of all fifteen MIMARU apartment hotels in Tokyo plus the Ascott, Oakwood and Citadines serviced apartments in September 2026, and kept only the room types whose official page states a maximum of six or more adults. Floor areas, bed configurations and washer information are as stated there; where a page does not say, we write “not stated”. Prices are deliberately not listed because they change daily. Some hotels here have no “See rates” link yet — we only link a booking page when we have the property’s verified identifier, and we would rather show the official site than guess.

The outliers: more than eight in one booking

MIMARU Tokyo Ueno Inaricho — a floor for twelve

The largest single booking we found anywhere in Tokyo. The hotel sells a “Floor Rental” of three rooms — one 38 m² and two 40 m² — as one unit for 12 adults plus 6 children, with six single beds, four futon and two sofa beds between them. It is a three-minute walk from Inaricho Station exit 3 on the Ginza Line, one stop from Ueno. This is the option for two or three families travelling together, or a multi-generation trip where everyone wants their own door. There is no in-room washer; the hotel has coin laundry. Official site

MIMARU Tokyo Nihombashi Suitengumae — nine

The Connecting Japanese Apartment for 9 is 79 m² with four single beds, three futon and a bunk bed, for 9 adults plus 4 children — the only nine-adult room type in this guide. Two minutes from Suitengumae Station on the Hanzomon Line, which is a quiet, genuinely residential part of central Tokyo with the Sumida river a short walk away. No in-room washer. Official site

Six people, and which ones have a washing machine

Rooms for six are the common case, and thirteen of the fifteen Tokyo MIMARU properties have at least one. The thing that actually separates them is laundry, because a family of six generates a wash a day. Only four of the six-person rooms have a washer-dryer in the room: MIMARU SUITES Tokyo Asakusa (Two-Bedroom Japanese Suite, 74 m², the largest of them), MIMARU SUITES Tokyo Nihombashi (Two-Bedroom Family Suite, 57–59 m², two minutes from Ningyocho), MIMARU Tokyo Ikebukuro (Two-Bedroom Family Suite, 58 m²) and MIMARU Tokyo Station East (Two-Bedroom, 53 m²). Everywhere else you are using coin laundry.

If floor space matters more than laundry, the roomiest sixes are MIMARU Tokyo Ueno Okachimachi’s One-Bedroom Apartment (64 m², six single beds, no futon), MIMARU Tokyo Ginza East’s Premium Japanese Apartment (63 m²) and MIMARU Tokyo Shinjuku West’s Deluxe Japanese Apartment (60 m²). What to check before you book a big room

  • Count the beds, not the capacity. An “8 adults” room can be eight single beds (Tokyo Station East, Ikebukuro) or four beds plus futon and sofa beds (Akasaka). If someone in the group cannot sleep on a futon on the floor, read the bed line.
  • A seventh birthday changes the room you need. Children aged 6 and under do not count towards MIMARU’s adult capacity; sevens do. A family of two adults and five children can be a “6 + 2” room one year and need an eight the next.
  • The biggest rooms have no in-room laundry. Every connecting apartment for 8 or 9 here uses coin laundry. If laundry matters more than space, take a two-bedroom suite for six with a washer instead.
  • Bathroom count is the quiet win. The connecting apartments have two bathrooms and two restrooms; a single 6-person apartment usually has one. With six people and one park day, that is the difference between leaving at 8 and leaving at 9.
  • These room types are scarce. Each property has only a handful, so they go first at peak times. If the dates are fixed, book the room before the flights.

Frequently asked questions

Can a family of six stay in one hotel room in Tokyo?

Yes, and there are more options than most booking sites surface. Thirteen of the fifteen MIMARU apartment hotels in Tokyo have at least one room type for 6 adults plus 2 children — typically 52–74 m² with four single beds and a bunk bed, or two single beds with futon. Outside MIMARU, Oakwood Premier Tokyo's 3 Bedroom Executive (110 m²) takes 6, and Ascott Marunouchi Tokyo's Marunouchi Suite (163 m²) and Stay Connected 3 Bedroom (129 m²) each take 6 with a seventh guest chargeable.

What is the largest hotel room in Tokyo for one family?

MIMARU Tokyo Ueno Inaricho rents a whole floor: three rooms (one 38 m² and two 40 m²) booked as one unit for 12 adults plus 6 children, with six single beds, four futon and two sofa beds. After that come the connecting apartments — MIMARU Tokyo Nihombashi Suitengumae's Connecting Japanese Apartment for 9 (79 m², 9 adults + 4 children) and the 76–82 m² connecting apartments for 8 adults + 4 children at Tokyo Station East, Akasaka, Ikebukuro, Ueno East and Hatchobori.

What counts as a child in these room capacities?

MIMARU states that up to 2 children aged 6 or under at check-in are not counted towards the adult capacity and may share a bed with an adult. So "8 adults + 4 children" means eight people in beds plus up to four small children sharing. A seventh birthday moves a child into the adult count, which can push a family of seven from an 8-person room into a 9-person one. Cribs are free for infants aged 2 and under and bed guards are free to borrow, both in limited numbers — contact the property in advance.

Which big Tokyo rooms have a washing machine in the room?

Very few of the large ones do. Among rooms for six or more, the in-room washer-dryers are at MIMARU SUITES Tokyo Asakusa (Two-Bedroom Japanese Suite, 74 m²), MIMARU SUITES Tokyo Nihombashi (Two-Bedroom Family Suite, 57–59 m²), MIMARU Tokyo Ikebukuro (Two-Bedroom Family Suite, 58 m²), MIMARU Tokyo Station East (Two-Bedroom, 53 m²) and Oakwood Premier Tokyo. Every connecting apartment for 8 or 9 in this guide has coin laundry instead — the biggest rooms and the in-room laundry do not overlap.

Is a connecting apartment better than two separate rooms?

For one family, usually yes: a connecting apartment is a single booking with a door between the two halves, so children can be put to bed on one side while adults stay up on the other, and there are two bathrooms rather than one. The catch is that these room types sell out first and none of them has an in-room washer. Two separate rooms are cheaper per person and easier to find at short notice, but you lose the shared living space and end up carrying a key between floors.

Where should a big family base itself in Tokyo?

The connecting apartments cluster in three areas. Around Tokyo Station — Hatchobori (MIMARU Tokyo Station East and MIMARU Tokyo Hatchobori are both two minutes from Hatchobori Station) — suits Shinkansen day trips to Kyoto or Nikko. Ueno and Asakusa (Ueno East, Ueno North, Ueno Okachimachi, Ueno Inaricho, Asakusa Station) put you on the Skyliner from Narita and in the flattest, most stroller-friendly part of old Tokyo. Ikebukuro and Shinjuku West are the Yamanote-line options if the trip is city-centred.
